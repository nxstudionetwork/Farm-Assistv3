Soil Testing Guide
Why Test Your Soil?
Soil testing helps you understand what nutrients your soil has and what it needs. This allows you to apply the right fertilizers in the right amounts, saving money and improving crop yields. Regular testing also helps track changes in soil health over time.
When to Test
Test your soil before planting a new crop and every 6 months during the growing season. For best results, test at the same time each year to compare results consistently.
How to Collect Samples
Collect soil from 6-8 different locations in your field, at a depth of 6-8 inches for most crops. Mix these samples together in a clean bucket. Avoid collecting from field edges, near fertilizer application areas, or recently fertilized spots.
Understanding Your Results
pH: Measures soil acidity. Most crops prefer 6.0-7.0. Below 6.0 is acidic, above 7.0 is alkaline.
Nitrogen (N): Essential for leaf growth. Low N causes yellowing leaves.
Phosphorus (P): Important for roots and flowering. Low P causes poor root development.
Potassium (K): Helps with overall plant health and disease resistance.
Organic Matter: Improves soil structure and water retention.

Irrigation Guide
When to Check Soil Moisture
Check soil moisture before irrigation to avoid over-watering. The best time to check is early morning. Insert a soil probe or use a moisture sensor at 6-8 inch depth. If the soil crumbles easily, it needs water. If it forms a ball, moisture is sufficient.
Identifying Water Stress
Signs of water stress include: leaves turning grayish-green, wilting during hot part of day, slow growth, and poor fruit/seed development. Early detection and proper irrigation can prevent significant yield loss.
Growth Stage & Water Needs
Different growth stages have different water requirements. Generally, crops need more water during flowering and fruit development stages compared to early vegetative growth. Adjust irrigation based on your crop's current growth stage.

Soil Care Guide
Organic Matter
Add organic matter through compost, farmyard manure, or green manure. This improves soil structure, water retention, and nutrient availability. Aim for 2-3% organic matter in most agricultural soils.
Crop Rotation
Rotate crops with different nutrient needs to maintain soil fertility and break pest cycles. Include legumes in rotation to naturally add nitrogen to the soil.
pH Management
If soil is too acidic (pH < 6.0), consider adding lime. If too alkaline (pH > 7.5), sulfur or organic matter can help. Maintain proper pH for optimal nutrient availability.
